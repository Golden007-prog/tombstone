Large engineering orgs keep re-proposing things they already rejected, because the rejection was never a thing you could look up. This is Kubernetes' own governance record — every enhancement proposal, every rejected alternative inside it, every ownership file, and the project's own record of who stepped back from approving.

Turn FalkorDB off and here is exactly what stops working

    

      “Has this been tried?” becomes grep over 688 markdown files — which is what
      contributors already have, and why the problem exists. The rejected alternative has no
      identity, so you cannot count how often an idea was refused, and you cannot return
      nothing: a vector index always hands back its k nearest rows, which is how a
      plausible wrong answer gets made. “Nobody named here still holds approval authority” is the
      absence of an edge, and absence is not something a similarity score can express.
      And the ranking of who held the most connective authority before stepping back is
      betweenness centrality — a whole-graph property no traversal, join or embedding can
      compute. There is no in-memory mirror and no cached fallback in this repo. With the
      database off, it fails loudly.
